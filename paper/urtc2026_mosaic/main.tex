\begin{abstract}
Emotion recognition is widely proposed as assistive technology for autistic
children, and papers on public autism facial-expression archives report up to
97.95\% accuracy. We audit one such archive. It ships two overlapping dataset
trees, 21.4\% of its files are byte-identical duplicates, and 119 duplicate
groups carry contradictory labels on pixel-identical images. Holding features
and classifier fixed while changing how train and test are separated,
five-category balanced accuracy spans 0.346--0.750; duplicate leakage explains
little of that---5.0 points, and 2.1 for a nearest-neighbour memoriser, neither
distinguishable from zero here. The contradictions have a degenerate geometry:
of the 103 groups spanning a mapped emotion, every one involves
\texttt{sadness}, and a single boundary absorbs 102 of them.
Controls then refute our own prior three-zone framework, which absorbs none of
them because it cuts where disagreement peaks, and whose apparent stability
advantage proves generic to class count. Chance-corrected under a group-aware
split, a binary non-negative/negative target scores 0.780 against 0.590 for
three zones and 0.625 for five categories. This is a measurement audit, not a
deployable classifier, and not a claim about any child's internal state.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
benchmark integrity, data leakage, label noise, affective computing, autism
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
Roughly 1 in 31 eight-year-olds in the United States is diagnosed with autism
spectrum disorder (ASD)~\cite{shaw2025}. Because emotional expression in
autistic people is frequently misread by non-autistic observers---the
\emph{double empathy problem}~\cite{milton2012}---automated emotion recognition is
repeatedly proposed as a communication aid.

The literature on this task is in an unusual state. Papers evaluating on public
autism facial-expression archives report accuracies from
80.0\%~\cite{radocaj2025} to 97.95\%~\cite{hanumantharayappa2026}. Papers
evaluating on independently collected, consented clinical corpora mostly report
less: 40\% for a commercial analyser on the EMBOA autism
corpus~\cite{kiejdo2025} and 57.95--64.92\% for four commercial classifiers on
parent-reported autistic children~\cite{kalantarian2020}. The comparison needs care:
Grossard \emph{et al.}~\cite{grossard2020} reach 82.05\% within-population on
lab-collected video, inside the public-archive band, and 66.43\% across
populations---so part of any gap is population transfer rather than provenance.
What remains unexplained is the top of the public-archive band, which no
consented corpus approaches.

That correlation has become hard to ignore. Publishers have retracted work built
on scraped autism face datasets, including the source paper for the archive we
audit~\cite{talaat2023retracted} and a facial emotion recognition
paper~\cite{gungor2025retracted}, on the grounds that images were collected from
the internet without documented diagnosis, ethical oversight, or guardian
consent. That established a \emph{provenance} failure. It did not establish
whether the reported numbers were also measurement artifacts.

This paper supplies that measurement: an integrity audit of the archive as
shipped (Sec.~\ref{sec:archive}); a protocol ablation with a nearest-neighbour
reference memoriser (Sec.~\ref{sec:ablation}); a partition analysis locating the
label contradictions, with a permutation null (Sec.~\ref{sec:zones}); and a
control experiment that refutes our own three-zone hypothesis, leaving a
narrower claim that survives it (Sec.~\ref{sec:beyond}). We audit an instrument
rather than build one, make no claim about any child's internal state, and treat
every label as a filing decision about an image rather than ground truth about a
person.

\section{Related Work}
\textbf{Cross-population transfer is established} by Grossard \emph{et
al.}~\cite{grossard2020} and, with twelve CNNs on an adjacent population, by
Gaya-Morey \emph{et al.}~\cite{gayamorey2026}. We do not claim that finding; we
ask whether the benchmarks now used to measure it can support the measurement.

\textbf{Benchmark contamination is a mature field.} Kapoor and
Narayanan~\cite{kapoor2023} catalogue eight leakage types across 294 papers;
Barz and Denzler~\cite{barz2020} found 3.3\% and 10\% duplicate rates in CIFAR
test sets; and Northcutt \emph{et al.}~\cite{northcutt2021} showed label
errors reorder model rankings. We adopt the taxonomy of Ramos \emph{et
al.}~\cite{ramos2025}---\emph{hard} leakage for identical images across a split,
\emph{soft} for near duplicates, \emph{intra-dataset} for overlap within one
dataset's splits---and the notions of \emph{augmented
duplicates}~\cite{adimoolam2026} and \emph{synthesis
leakage}~\cite{apicella2025}. None of it examines a clinical affect dataset.
Birhane and Prabhu~\cite{birhane2021} argue scraped corpora containing minors
are ethically defective at collection.

\textbf{Contamination in this archive has been asserted, not measured.} The
authors of the FERAC derivative~\cite{ferac2024} reported ``class overlap and
image duplication'' here, removed duplicates, and dropped two classes as
unrecoverable, but reported no detection method, threshold, duplicate rate,
leakage rate, or effect of cleaning. Two groups have now flagged the same
archive; what remains open is the quantity.

\textbf{Why the labels might be the problem.} Barrett \emph{et
al.}~\cite{barrett2019} challenge the premise that emotional state is readable
from facial configuration at all, and Keating \emph{et al.}~\cite{keating2026}
find autistic and non-autistic expressions differ in which muscles carry each
emotion. If expressions are ambiguous under a non-autistic reading, forcing them
into mutually exclusive categories should produce contradictions concentrated
where categories are closest.

\section{The Archive}
\label{sec:archive}
We audit the public Kaggle archive associated
with~\cite{talaat2023retracted}.\footnote{Slug
\texttt{fatmamtalaat/}\-\texttt{autistic-}\-\texttt{children-}\-\texttt{emotions-}\-\texttt{dr-fatma-m-talaat}.
We name it for reproducibility while noting it distributes images of minors of
disputed provenance; readers should weigh that before downloading.} An earlier run of ours retained 1{,}672 files
after excluding \texttt{surprise} and the conflicted groups. All 1{,}429 distinct
SHA-256 digests covering those files are present in the fresh download, so the
archive has not changed under us. The audit here covers all 2{,}258 files
(1{,}774 distinct images). Statistics below come from the vendor's own directory layout, before
any re-splitting: this is what a downstream user receives.

\textbf{Two overlapping datasets ship as one.} The archive contains two
top-level trees (Table~\ref{tab:trees}) with different class balances and
splits, sharing 323 unique images---45.0\% of the smaller tree's unique images. Published descriptions of this
dataset disagree on its size, and only one of the reported figures approximately
matches a tree (830 against the smaller tree's 833 files); two trees with overlapping contents are at least a candidate
explanation, though we cannot reconstruct every published count.

\textbf{One file in five is a duplicate.} Of 2{,}258 labelled images only
1{,}774 are distinct by SHA-256; 484 files (21.4\%) are byte-identical copies,
in groups reaching size 5. Perceptual hashing at Hamming distance 4 adds 163
near-duplicate components. A filename suffix (\texttt{\_aug\_N}) identifies
1{,}020 augmentation families, 335 larger than one, so part of the archive is
synthetic variation on fewer source images---synthesis
leakage~\cite{apicella2025}, and identifiable from filenames alone without any
image analysis.

\textbf{Pixel-identical images carry contradictory labels.} Under the vendor's
six categories, 119 of the 346 duplicate groups (34\%, 327 files) are filed
under more than one emotion; dropping \texttt{surprise}, which no valence zone
covers, leaves the 103 groups used from Sec.~\ref{sec:zones} onward. Establishing this needs no
external ground truth and no annotator panel, only a hash. It is narrower than
label-error detection~\cite{northcutt2021}---it finds only errors a duplicate
exposes, and cannot say which label is wrong---but it is assumption-free, and an
internal inconsistency of the artifact is not arguable.

\section{How Much of the Number Is the Protocol?}
\label{sec:ablation}
Contamination matters only if it moves results. We hold the features and the
classifier fixed and vary how train and test are separated. This is not a clean intervention and we do not present
it as one: training pools range from 695 to 1{,}772 files and test sets from 69
to 293, because that is what the archive's own splits give you. Seed dispersion
in Table~\ref{tab:ablation} excludes test-set sampling error, which is
${\approx}\pm0.06$ at $n=69$ and ${\approx}\pm0.03$ at $n=293$. The question is
what a reader learns from one published number, not what one isolated factor
contributes. Features are frozen \texttt{ViT-B/16}
embeddings~\cite{dosovitskiy2021}; the head is a one-hidden-layer MLP (256
units, ReLU, Adam, batch 64, at most 100 epochs). Early stopping selects the
epoch and the $L_2$ penalty from $\{10^{-5},10^{-4},10^{-3}\}$ on macro-F1 over
a 15\% slice \emph{carved out of the training split} with patience 12; the test
split is touched once, to score the selected model. Every protocol runs over ten
seeds~\cite{bouthillier2021}, each redrawing that validation slice. Because \texttt{surprise} has no zone, it is
dropped from \emph{both} arms, so the categorical arm is a five-way task over
exactly the images the zone arm sees and the two columns differ only in label
granularity.

We add a 1-nearest-neighbour classifier as a \emph{reference memoriser}. It
stores the training set exactly, so a test image whose byte-identical twin is in
training is recovered with certainty. It is not an upper bound over all models---a
fine-tuned network memorises and generalises, and we did not run one---but it is
the estimator most able to convert an exact duplicate into a correct prediction,
which makes it the sharpest available probe of how much these duplicates are
worth under this representation.

Table~\ref{tab:ablation} supports three claims and refutes a fourth---that
leakage explains the published figures---which we expected to make.

\textbf{The reported number is largely a choice.} Across five defensible
protocols on identical images, five-category balanced accuracy spans 0.346 to
0.750, a 40.4-point range. The protocols also differ in test-set size and
composition, so this is protocol plus sampling rather than protocol alone; the
smallest cells hold 69 images.
Seed dispersion is 2.8--5.2 points in this block,
several times smaller than the protocol spread even after allowing that the
range of five draws is itself about $2.3\sigma$; test-set sampling error at
$n=69$ is roughly $\pm0.06$. A reader given one number cannot tell where in
that band it sits.

\textbf{The zone collapse narrows the spread.} The same protocols span 23.8
points under three zones against 40.4 under five categories. Because the label
spaces have different chance floors we compare them chance-corrected as
$(\text{BA}-1/k)/(1-1/k)$. Sec.~\ref{sec:beyond} quantifies the reduction under
a single fixed split, where the controls also run, and shows it is not a
property of \emph{these} classes.

\textbf{Duplicate leakage is second-order at most.} Pooled random has a
byte-identical twin in training for 35.8\% of its test images and a family
member for 80.6\%, and exceeds the group-aware split by 5.0 points under five
categories and 0.8 under zones. Neither difference is detectable: combining
test-set standard errors at $n=180$ and $n=293$ gives ${\approx}0.047$ on the
difference, so the five-category gap is about $1.1\sigma$ and its 95\%
interval runs from about $-4$ to $+14$ points---it includes zero. We therefore
cannot detect a leakage effect, and equally cannot rule out one several times
larger than our point estimate. Note also that the group-aware arm holds out
less (180 against 293) and so trains on more data, which if anything makes 5.0
a floor rather than a ceiling. The reference memoriser gains
even less under five categories, 2.1 points. The exception is the target we go
on to recommend: under binary valence the gap is 3.5 points for the head and 5.7
for 1-NN, which chance-corrected is 7.0 and 11.4---larger than the five-category
gap of 6.2. Duplicates buy little on the hard task and more on the easy one,
which is the opposite of convenient for us. 1-NN also matches or beats the trained head on four of five five-category
protocols, which bounds how much the head is extracting beyond nearest-neighbour
structure and is a further reason to read these absolute values as a floor.

\textbf{Under frozen features, this contamination does not reach the published
band.} Under five categories the best value any protocol reaches, with any
estimator we ran, is 0.755---below the 80.0--97.95\% band reported on archives of this
family~\cite{radocaj2025,hanumantharayappa2026}. Those are plain accuracies and ours
balanced, which favours them here; our pooled-random accuracy is 0.721, which
does not change the conclusion. So \emph{exact-duplicate and
augmentation-family leakage, under frozen \texttt{ViT-B/16} features and our
re-splits}, does not by itself produce those figures. This is not a claim that contamination is
irrelevant: augmentation applied before splitting in downstream derivatives is
itself synthesis leakage~\cite{apicella2025} and would not appear in our
accounting. Small test sets, selection among runs, and fine-tuned networks on
contaminated splits remain untested candidates.

\section{Where the Contradictions Live}
\label{sec:zones}
The contradictions are not spread evenly across the label space, and locating
them turns out to matter more than counting them.

Our prior three-zone framework groups the vendor's categories as zone~1
\{\texttt{natural} (the vendor's label for neutral), \texttt{joy}\}, zone~2
\{\texttt{anger}, \texttt{fear}\}, zone~3 \{\texttt{sadness}\}, leaving
\texttt{surprise} unmapped. Restricting to the 1{,}952 files that receive a
zone, we ask for each coarsening of the five categories how many byte-identical
duplicate groups still straddle a block boundary. A coarsening that absorbs many
contradictions is one whose boundaries the filing process respected.

\textbf{Every contradiction involves one category.} The pairwise structure
(Fig.~\ref{fig:conflicts}) is degenerate: \texttt{anger}--\texttt{sadness}
accounts for 77 groups, \texttt{fear}--\texttt{sadness} for 25, and
\texttt{natural}--\texttt{sadness} for 1. Every other pair, including
\texttt{anger}--\texttt{fear} and \texttt{natural}--\texttt{joy}, has none. So
splitting positive/neutral from negative leaves one conflicted group out of 346,
and a permutation null preserving the zone marginals and duplicate-group
structure sharpens that.
The null over-predicts total conflicted
\emph{files} by 1.9$\times$, so we condition on the 280 that occur. Counting
files, 113 are expected to carry a positive/neutral label against 1 observed---a
depletion of at least an order of magnitude, though resting on a single event and
so imprecise---while negative-affect files are \emph{enriched} about 1.7-fold
(279 against 167). Table~\ref{tab:conflicts} counts groups; this counts files. The filing process
separated valence almost perfectly and anger, fear, and sadness not at all.

\textbf{The three-zone partition absorbs none of it.} This is a negative result
about our own prior framework, and Fig.~\ref{fig:conflicts} shows why: the zones
put \texttt{anger} and \texttt{fear} together and \texttt{sadness} apart, so the
zone-2/zone-3 boundary severs both heavy edges and no zero-weight one. Among the
15 same-shape partitions it ties for worst, while grouping \texttt{anger} with
\texttt{sadness} absorbs 77 of 103.

We do not call this annotator disagreement: the archive is a scrape with no
documented labelling procedure, and one image retrieved under two queries and
written into two folders explains it as well. Either way the consequence for a
model is identical, and it is the consequence our evidence supports.

\section{What This Does and Does Not License}
\label{sec:beyond}
\textbf{A stability argument for zones does not survive its control.} From
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} the chance-corrected protocol range narrows from 0.505
under five categories to 0.357 under three zones, a 29.3\% reduction, which
reads as support for zones. To test whether \emph{these} three classes are
responsible we re-ran the same five protocols over all 15 partitions of the five
categories into blocks of 2, 2, and 1---the zones' own block shape---on one
fixed split with five seeds. There the five-category baseline is 0.482 and zones
give 0.404 (16.1\%; it differs from 29.3\% because the control uses a fixed
split and five seeds, so only within-run comparison is meaningful). But the 15
partitions span 0.325 to 0.628, a deliberately valence-crossing partition does
\emph{better} at 0.391, and zones rank 5th---above median, unremarkable, and on
a statistic unlikely to be stable under reseeding. Fewer classes buys stability;
these classes buy nothing extra. We report this because we expected the
opposite.

\textbf{What survives is a granularity claim, and it is sharper.} The evidence in
Sec.~\ref{sec:zones} is specific to one boundary: the reproducible distinction
separates \{\texttt{natural}, \texttt{joy}\} from negative affect---strictly,
non-negative versus negative rather than valence proper, since the vendor's
\texttt{natural} is neutral---and the anger/fear/sadness distinctions are not
reproducible at all.
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} bears this out where it matters. Under the group-aware
split, chance-corrected so the three label spaces are commensurable, binary
valence scores 0.780 against 0.625 for five categories and 0.590 for three
zones. The zone collapse does not even improve on the five-way task once the
floor is removed; only the valence collapse does, and it does so at exactly the
boundary the conflict analysis identifies as reliable. So the archive supports a valence
decision well and finer ones poorly. That is consistent with the fine labels
being unreliable, though it does not separate unreliable labels from a simply
harder task; both point to a binary target
rather than the three-zone scheme we and others have used---or, if three levels
are wanted for intervention, for a partition that does not cut between anger and
sadness. Coarsening does reduce protocol sensitivity, but not monotonically and not in
proportion to how good the target is: chance-corrected, the range is 0.505 for
five categories, 0.357 for zones, and 0.410 for valence. Coarsening changes
\emph{what} is asked; it does not reliably reduce how much the answer depends on
the split.

We ran no caregiver study, so the actionability that motivates zones is not
something we can speak to.

\textbf{Context beyond the face.} Under our strictest split the five-category
result is 0.700, and since our groups are duplicate families rather than
children that is an upper bound on cleanliness. Barrett \emph{et
al.}~\cite{barrett2019} argue facial configuration underdetermines emotional
state in principle. If the face reliably yields only valence, everything
clinically interesting must come from elsewhere.

Two directions follow, neither tested here. A face-only autism-specific model
needs autism-specific face data---the resource whose acquisition produced the
retractions above---whereas a vision-language model supplies scene and
interaction context without any such corpus. And microexpression analysis, the
usual proposal for recovering finer states, needs frame rates and resolutions
that in-the-wild capture of a child does not deliver.

\section{Limitations and Ethics}
This audits one archive and does not establish prevalence. Duplicate detection is
hash-based and misses re-encoded or cropped duplicates, so our rates are lower
bounds, and the Hamming-4 threshold was not validated against hand-labelled
pairs. The archive supplies no subject identities, so our groups are duplicate
and augmentation families rather than people; a subject-disjoint split would be
stricter. Our features come from a web-pretrained backbone, so we cannot exclude
that the encoder has seen these images---a caveat our ``0\% leakage'' rows do
not escape. The reference memoriser characterises contamination under
\emph{these} features only, and no fine-tuned network was run.

\textbf{Ethics.} This is secondary analysis of an already-public dataset, with no
interaction with human subjects and no re-identification attempt; we sought no
institutional determination and assert none. We report only aggregate
statistics, released no per-image data, and deleted the image corpus after
hashing and feature extraction. We make no claim that any depicted child is
autistic, that any label reflects a felt emotion, or that any system should be
deployed on this basis; where Sec.~\ref{sec:zones} cites findings about autistic
expression it motivates a hypothesis rather than asserting that this archive
depicts diagnosed children. Provenance is disputed and the license unstated, so
we redistribute nothing.

\section{Conclusion}
This archive ships two overlapping copies sharing 45\% of the smaller tree's
unique images, is 21.4\% duplicated, and contains 119 groups of pixel-identical
images with contradictory labels. Changing how train and test are separated
moves five-category balanced accuracy across a 40.4-point band, while duplicate
leakage moves it by an undetectable 5.0 points under frozen features---a
fine-tuned encoder, which can memorise in a way frozen features cannot, remains
untested. Either way, one number here says little about a model.

The contradictions have a geometry: one boundary absorbs 102 of 103, and
cross-valence conflict is depleted by at least an order of magnitude against a
marginal-preserving null, while conflict within negative affect is if anything
enriched. Our own three-zone
framework absorbs none of them---it cuts between anger and sadness, where
disagreement peaks---and its stability advantage proves generic to class count.
Chance-corrected, only the binary non-negative/negative target improves on the
five-way task. This archive supports that decision and little else. Future
benchmarks here should report a duplicate audit, a group-aware split, and
chance-corrected metrics as a condition of being believed---and, given how this
one was assembled, should not be built by scraping.
